Indonesia is an island country between the Indian and Pacific oceans. Its thousands of islands include Sumatra, Java, Sulawesi, and parts of Borneo and New Guinea. The country's many communities speak different languages and share a long history of trade and cultural exchange.

Etymology

The name Indonesia combines Greek roots referring to India and islands. It came into use during the nineteenth century and was later adopted by people seeking a shared national identity.

Early history

People have lived in the archipelago for a very long time. Sea routes connected its communities with traders from other parts of Asia. Kingdoms such as Srivijaya and Majapahit became influential centres of commerce and culture.

Colonial era

European traders arrived in search of spices, and the Dutch eventually established colonial rule. Indonesia declared independence in 1945, followed by a struggle for international recognition.
